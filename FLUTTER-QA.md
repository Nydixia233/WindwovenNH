# Trailcloth 0.1.2 — flutter QA

## Implementation

A broad travelling pressure wave and a faster wave that pushes the two edges in opposite directions are applied across the cape. A slow varying pulse gives runs of stronger and weaker billow instead of repeating a single whole-sheet swing. The waves become stronger toward the hem. Input speed (including a weighted vertical component) sets a filtered envelope; a cape-specific phase avoids synchronized flaps. This is a visual cloth-force model, not a fluid simulation. Walking speed does not excite flutter; underwater coupling and existing damping keep it much weaker.

The two existing solver substeps, cloth/torso constraints, LODs and render interpolation remain in place. The wave trigonometry is evaluated at most once per simulated cape per tick; spatial terms are shared and precomputed for both grids. There are no added solver passes or render-time noise. The warmed-up standalone solver + mesh loop still allocated **0 bytes over 1,500 iterations** in the local test; this does not measure whole-client allocations or guarantee unchanged frame times.

## Regression

Both grids were driven at a steady 0.90 blocks/tick with normal armor. The data below measure cape **shape**, not frames or frame rate. Measurements are peak-to-peak after the initial motion had settled.

| Grid | 0.1.1 hem height range | 0.1.2 in air | 0.1.2 with underwater flag | 0.1.2 left minus right corner in air |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 5 × 13 | <0.001 blocks | 0.350 blocks | 0.067 blocks | −0.180 to +0.189 blocks |
| 3 × 7 | <0.001 blocks | 0.330 blocks | 0.064 blocks | −0.189 to +0.193 blocks |

The tests verify that the motion is primarily in the lower cloth and changes curvature, with both corners leading at different times. They also check interpolation, finite positions, armor clearance, bounded stretch, no lockstep between separately seeded capes, reset, fade after braking, and flutter in constant ascent and descent. A lower forward speed of 0.32 blocks/tick gives a much smaller 0.044-block peak-to-peak hem range. At 0.18 blocks/tick, both grids settle without ongoing flutter.

## GTNH in-game replay

In a disposable GTNH 2.9.0 Prism client (Java 25 and Angelica 2.2.43 OpenGL), a scripted actor wearing an iron chestplate followed the same line at 0.90 blocks/tick and then braked. The camera followed the actor from behind at an angle. Shaders-off captures match 0.1.1 against 0.1.2; a third 0.1.2 run used Complementary Reimagined r5.9.3 + Euphoria Patches 1.10.5. The 30 evenly spaced captures per run sample 3 seconds of constant speed. The 0.1.1 silhouette stayed almost straight. In 0.1.2 the outer hem alternately curved, twisted and exposed both faces, and returned to a draped shape after braking. The effect remained visible with shaders on; the sampled frames showed no visible duplicate cape or obvious iron-chestplate clipping.

All three runs recorded 155 cloth steps over 155 sampled client ticks and no distance fallbacks. The shader capture submitted the mesh for both render passes without doubling the solve rate. The capture CSVs include the fixture's screenshot overhead and are not performance benchmarks. This preview was not requalified for the full 0.1.0 performance and network gates. Local evidence is under `build/measurements/0.1.2/`.

The replay tested the reobfuscated `trailcloth-0.1.2.jar`, SHA-256 `5024bf785d722b98ff60070ffd9e8d7cae8198212eddc7208cff17a29c3b67bd`. The cape swatch was generated by the fixture; game resources, shaders, captures and the fixture itself are not bundled in the production binary. This preview is staged locally only.
